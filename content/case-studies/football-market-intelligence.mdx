## The claim

I built a model to price football match outcomes and backtested it against closing bookmaker odds. The
best configuration I ran returned +6.72% ROI over 118 bets. Most of the configurations I ran lost
money. In the 2023/24 season the model placed no bets at all.

The claim of this post is that the correct summary of that work is "the model does not beat the
market", and that the +6.72% is not evidence against this summary but an illustration of why single
favourable slices are untrustworthy.

This is the most useful thing I have to say about modelling, and it happens to come from the project
that worked least well.

## Why the obvious approach fails

The obvious approach is to search over configurations, find the one that is profitable, and report it.
Nothing about that process is fraudulent in a narrow sense. Every number is real, computed correctly,
on data that was genuinely held out. It is still worthless, for two reasons.

The first is multiplicity. If you evaluate enough thresholds, staking rules, market selections and
feature sets, some of them will be profitable on any finite sample, including a sample generated by
noise. Reporting the maximum of a search without reporting the search is reporting an order statistic
and calling it an estimate. The relevant question is not "was this configuration profitable" but "how
many did I try, and what did the distribution look like". Here the distribution was mostly negative.

The second is sample size. 118 bets is small. Betting returns are heavy-tailed and highly variable:
the standard error on ROI over 118 flat-staked bets is wide enough that +6.72% is entirely consistent
with a true edge of zero, or a negative one. Quoting it to two decimal places, as I just did, implies
a precision that the sample does not support. I quote it precisely because it is the committed number,
not because it is meaningfully distinguishable from break-even.

The benchmark that matters here is unusual and worth stating plainly: it is not another model. It is
the closing line, which is the aggregate of every informed opinion in the market, and which is very
hard to beat because it is priced to be.

## What I did instead

I kept the losing configurations. The result that ships is the whole distribution, not the maximum of
it, and the honest headline is the negative one.

The other decision was structural. In the 2023/24 season, the model placed zero bets. That is not a
bug and I did not patch around it. The selection criteria simply found nothing that met the threshold,
which is the correct behaviour for a system that is supposed to bet only when it believes it has an
edge. A model that always finds something to bet on is a model whose threshold is decorative.

That zero-bet season is also the most informative single fact in the backtest. It suggests the
relationship the model relies on was not present in that period, which is the signature of a structural
break rather than ordinary variance. A profitable backtest that quietly spans such a break is
substantially less trustworthy than one that visibly stops trading through it.

## The result, next to its benchmark

The benchmark is break-even against the closing line, which is 0% ROI before costs and negative after
them.

| Configuration | Bets | ROI |
|---|---|---|
| Best (market logistic regression, Pinnacle, threshold 0.05) | 118 | +6.72% |
| Most other configurations | varies | negative |
| 2023/24 season, all configurations | 0 | no bets placed |

Read as a whole: one configuration cleared the benchmark on a small sample, the majority did not, and
the system declined to trade for an entire season. That is not a market edge. It is what an honest
backtest of a model without an edge looks like.

## What I would not claim

- I do not claim a tradeable edge exists. The best configuration's +6.72% over 118 bets is not evidence
  of one, and this post should not be read as suggesting otherwise.
- I do not claim the +6.72% is statistically distinguishable from zero. On 118 bets it is not.
- I have not published a confidence interval or a multiplicity correction over the configuration search,
  so the honest position is that the best number is an upper bound produced by searching.
- No money was staked on this. It is a backtest against historical closing odds, with all the optimism
  that implies relative to live execution, slippage and limits.
